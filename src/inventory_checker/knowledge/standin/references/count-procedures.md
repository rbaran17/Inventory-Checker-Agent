# Evaluating management's count instructions
> refs: ISA 501.4(a)(i); AU-C 501; AS 2510
> topics: planning, procedures

Before or at the start of the count, review the client's written count instructions. Matters to look
for include: control over count tags or sheets (pre-numbered, issued and returned under control);
procedures to identify and segregate damaged, obsolete, slow-moving and consigned goods; how goods are
counted (teams, blind counts, second counts); control over movements during the count (a freeze, or
cutoff procedures if movements continue); and the cutoff of receiving and shipping.

# Observing the performance of count procedures
> refs: ISA 501.4(a)(ii); AU-C 501; AS 2510
> topics: procedures

Observe whether count teams follow the instructions: tags placed on every item or location, counts
recorded in ink, recounts where expected, no movement of goods in counted areas, and segregation of
damaged or consigned goods. Record each deviation and its likely effect, and tell the client's count
supervisor promptly so it can be corrected during the count.

# Movement freeze and segregation
> refs: AU-C 501; ISA 501; AS 2510
> topics: procedures, cutoff

Movements during the count increase the risk of double counting or omission. If production or shipping
continues, confirm how moving goods are identified and kept out of, or included in, the count. Goods
held for others (consignment in) should be segregated and excluded; goods held elsewhere (consignment
out, third-party warehouses) need separate evidence.
